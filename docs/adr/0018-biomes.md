# 18. Biomes as rider-side, cosmetic world skins

Date: 2026-11-02 · Status: accepted

## Context

Riders asked for seasons: a winter valley, a Halloween night. A season must
change the *shape* of the world's scenery, not just its palette — snowy
spruces, banks along the road, chalets instead of farmhouses — and it must be
possible to add more without textures, glTF models or a build step: the world
is generated by hand in `client/public/js/view3d/` and drawn by a custom
WebGL renderer with flat, vertex-coloured meshes.

Routes on one map share one world (ADR 0010): terrain, road network, villages
and the café/gym are the same for every rider, and riders meet at those
places. A season picked by one rider must not move anything another rider
sees, or two riders would talk about "the village" while looking at different
roads.

## Decision

- **A biome is a rider setting, not map data.** `openride.biome` in
  `localStorage`, like time of day. `?biome=<id>` previews one without saving;
  the default is `temperate`.
- **A biome is pure data and pure functions.** `view3d/biomes/<id>.js` (or a
  `<id>/` folder for a season with several parts) exports an object with the
  road/arch colours, the terrain shading rule, the flora placement hooks, the
  model factories and palette. The registry (`biomes/index.js`) resolves an
  id; unknown ids fall back to the default. No assets, no I/O, so it runs in
  the world worker and under `node --test`.
- **A biome is cosmetic only.** These stay identical in every biome and are
  guarded by tests:
  - terrain heights and coast,
  - road centreline, width, lanes, markings' offsets,
  - zone layout: villages, rivers and bridges, forests, cliffs,
  - start/finish, climb and sprint arches, the flamme rouge,
  - café and gym placement, the gym's mat and its eight workout spots,
  - `overview` (the menu camera) and the sea level.

  Scenery — trees, bushes, rocks, house/barn/church massing, props, ground
  cover — may change shape and colour freely. Houses stay inside their
  village lot and never reach the road.
- **Model factories live on the biome** (`biome.models.house(spec)`, `.cafe()`,
  `.gym(opts)`, …), so a season can replace a model outright. Features key
  their model caches by `biome.id`, so two seasons in one worker session do
  not share shapes.
- **Flora and the crowd are biome hooks too.** `biome.flora.tree` draws each
  season's trees, and `biome.people.model(pose)` draws the crowd's two poses;
  `biome.people.motion` tells the view how to play them (`jump` for people,
  `clap` for winter's penguins). Both are built in the world like any other
  model, so the view animates plain data across the worker boundary.
- **Rebuild in the worker.** Changing the season rebuilds the scene through
  `buildScene(route, others, { network, biome })`; the old world keeps
  rendering until the new one arrives, then the old view is disposed before
  the new one is uploaded (two full maps at once would double vertex memory:
  ~175 MB for the valley map). The start menu's stage uses the same key so a
  season picked there carries into the ride.

## Consequences

- Two riders on the same road may see different seasons. That is intended:
  race position comes from the server, scenery is decorative.
- Adding a season means one module plus a registry entry, a label in both
  dictionaries and a test that its layout invariants hold. Its triangle
  budget must stay near the temperate one (long routes are capped per km).
- A shared world is still built once per client; a season change costs one
  world build (≈0.6 s for Rolling Hills, ≈2.4 s for the full valley map).
- Functional models (café, gym) keep their footprint and the sign/window
  meshes the game uses, so their spots and workout positions stay exact; the
  gym itself is identical in every biome, while the café may change shape
  around the same terrace.
